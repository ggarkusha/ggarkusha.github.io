How to operate the experiments

The 3D view

  • Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, shift-drag (or two-finger drag) to pan. Double-click or Reset view restores the camera.
  • With the view focused: arrow keys rotate, + and − zoom, 0 resets.
  • Click a zero, sphere or ring to read the point of ℂⁿ it stands for. In a stack, the complete tuple is highlighted. The tuple selector also reaches points hidden by overlaps. In the first experiment a click draws the local picture of the framing.
  • Expand enlarges the view; Esc closes it.

The four experiments

  • One framed correspondence: choose a framing f(z), move its zeros, and read the degree.
  • Composition and shuffles: the identities in simplicial degree 1, where all sphere labels are the unique nonbasepoint label; composition through Y = 𝔸¹; and the Σₙ-action. Play runs the permutation.
  • Stabilisation: add σ-layers and watch equivariance (4) and bilinearity (8). Each correspondence carries one common cut word on its support; general componentwise labels are not implemented.
  • Homotopy over Δ¹: zeros as braids in ℂ × [0,1]; slide s, or sweep it.

C.2 Commutativity is a shuffle

The ring structure on is the product of framed correspondences, so commutativity (Prop. 4.7, Thm. 3.2(2)) says : exchanging the factors moves the layers of past the layers of . Each point keeps its coordinates and changes only its layer. Because carries no coefficients other than the maps themselves, nothing else is needed, and all identities of the paper hold on the nose. E. Limitations

Algebraic justification. The framings are explicit polynomials. Products, conjugation and bonding use the formulas in A.4, and their identities follow algebraically by associativity and reordering the relevant coordinate blocks. Monicity proves finite flatness of the valid families and of each displayed second framing over . The chosen complete-intersection zero schemes have the stated lengths.

Numerical diagnostics. The implementation compares framings at eight deterministic pseudorandom complex points in double precision and compares listed zero multisets after rounding to . Winding numbers are sampled on small circles. Passing these tests does not prove a polynomial identity, establish the completeness of an arbitrary root list, or test the neighbourhood equivalence relation. No historical test-suite count is asserted here.

Choices. The catalogue contains polynomials with roots known in closed form. Multiplicities are supplied from their algebraic structure. For the displayed fibre products the second polynomial is monic, so the local length is the first local length times the second fibre length; this is not a numerical multiplicity algorithm. Objects are a point and , and there are at most four complex-coordinate layers. Composition and module experiments use simplicial degree 1. The stabilisation experiment implements only uniformly labelled supports and nonbasepoint cut words, not the full simplicial mapping spaces or their face and degeneracy operators.

Graphical approximation. The surface is a sampled triangle mesh. The real parameter slice of a family is drawn by sampled polygonal curves. Heights, hues and colours are display choices. A permutation animation moves coordinate vertices to their destination layers; intermediate frames have no categorical meaning. Indexed tuple data are retained, while an unselected union of overlapping polylines may conceal the grouping.

Not shown. General étale neighbourhoods and their equivalence relation; the empty correspondence, which is the basepoint; general componentwise simplicial labels; the motivic topology; geometric realisation of ; Theorem 4.15; and Section 6. All displayed polynomial examples, including composition through , have algebraic representatives with the identity étale neighbourhood.

What the experiments show

  • A level-one framed correspondence is a degree- local picture. The winding of the framing at each zero is its multiplicity, and the total is the degree.
  • Composition factors through zero sets. For a point it is a product with multiplicities multiplying; through it is a fibre product, and degeneracies of the fibre appear as merged zeros.
  • In this instance commutativity is a shuffle and nothing else. The commutativity of , the interchange law and the module law are each an exchange of blocks of layers.
  • The structure maps are visible. The iterated map is equivariant because is invariant under , and the shuffle of the bilinearity diagram is the movement of the σ-layer past the layers of .
  • Homotopy preserves the degree, and finiteness is why. Zeros may collide inside a finite flat family; they may not escape.
